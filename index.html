Common threats

Recognize attacks before they strike.

Spotting early warning signs turns potential disasters into harmless blips.

Phishing

Emails or messages impersonating trusted sources to steal logins or payment data.

  • Check sender domains and spelling.
  • Hover links to preview destinations.
  • When unsure, contact the source directly.

Social Engineering

Attackers manipulate emotions—urgency, fear, curiosity—to push risky actions.

  • Pause before reacting to threats or prizes.
  • Verify identities using a second channel.
  • Limit personal info you share publicly.

Malware & Ransomware

Malicious software that locks, leaks, or destroys data after unsafe downloads.

  • Keep OS and apps updated.
  • Avoid pirated software and unknown USBs.
  • Maintain offline/cloud backups.

Unsafe Wi‑Fi

Public networks can expose traffic or enable man-in-the-middle attacks.

  • Avoid sensitive tasks on public Wi‑Fi.
  • Use VPN and HTTPS-only mode.
  • Forget networks you don’t trust.

Identity Theft

Use of stolen PII to open accounts, file taxes, or impersonate you online.

  • Freeze credit where available.
  • Use unique emails or aliases for signups.
  • Review statements and alerts monthly.

Cloud Misconfig

Open buckets, weak IAM, or default creds exposing sensitive data.

  • Disable public access by default.
  • Use MFA + least privilege.
  • Enable audit logs and alerts.

Safety tips

Turn good habits into muscle memory.

Simple routines drastically reduce risk—no jargon required.

✔

Enable MFA everywhere

Add an extra lock so stolen passwords can’t be reused.

✔

Turn on alerts

Get notified about logins, payments, and account changes.

✔

Install from official stores

Avoid sideloaded apps that may contain hidden malware.

✔

Separate work & personal

Use different profiles to isolate credentials.

✔

Verify before you share

Never send sensitive info over DM without confirming identity.

✔

Backup routinely

Keep 3 copies: device, external drive, and cloud.

✔

Auto-update devices

Patch browsers, OS, and routers to close known holes.

✔

Review app permissions

Remove mic/camera/location access you don’t need.

Password security

Strong, unique, and stored safely.

Weak or reused passwords cause most breaches. Fix that in minutes.

Recipe for a strong password

Make cracking attempts impractical by design.

  • At least 14 characters (more is better).
  • Mix upper, lower, numbers, and symbols.
  • Avoid words, quotes, or personal info.
  • Use a unique password per site.

Use a password manager

It creates and stores unique passwords, auto-fills them, and syncs securely across devices.

  • Enable passkeys where available for phishing-resistant logins.
  • Turn on breach alerts and auto-rotate when supported.

Real-life cases

Lessons from incidents that made headlines.

Real stories show how small mistakes become big problems—and how to avoid them.

2024Deepfake Scam

Voice-cloned CEO authorizes $25M transfer

Attackers used AI-generated audio to mimic an executive and pressure finance staff.

  • Use call-back verification for large payments.
  • Train staff to spot urgency + secrecy patterns.
2023Ransomware

Hospital forced to divert patients

Unpatched VPN appliance was exploited; backups were outdated.

  • Patch external systems rapidly.
  • Test backups and keep an offline copy.
2022Phishing

Payroll portal credentials stolen

Lookalike domain captured employee logins.

  • Use MFA and domain monitoring.
  • Enable browser password alerts.
2021Supply Chain

Compromised update delivers malware

Users installed a trusted app update that was backdoored upstream.

  • Verify signatures and SBOMs.
  • Monitor outbound connections after updates.
